Strengths and weaknesses:

- *Mean baseline* (regression): Predicts the training-set mean grade for everyone; the reference to beat. Uses no information about the student.
- *Linear regression* (regression): Transparent additive model; stable with few observations; no tuning. Assumes additive, linear effects. Nominal attributes are fully one-hot encoded, so individual coefficients are not uniquely identified (the least-squares solver returns the minimum-norm solution); predictions are unaffected.
- *Decision tree* (regression): Captures thresholds and interactions; readable when shallow. High variance: small changes in the data can change the tree.
- *Random forest* (regression): Averages many trees: lower variance than one tree, models interactions. Less transparent; predictions are shrunk towards the mean at the extremes.
- *Majority-class baseline* (classification): Predicts 'not at risk' for everyone; shows what accuracy means here. Identifies no at-risk student (recall 0).
- *Logistic regression* (classification): Transparent, well-calibrated probabilities; regularisation handles 46 inputs. Linear decision boundary on the encoded inputs.
- *Decision tree* (classification): Readable rules; captures thresholds such as grade cut-offs. High variance; coarse probability estimates (leaf proportions).
- *k-nearest neighbours* (classification): No model assumptions; local similarity between students. No class weighting, so the minority at-risk class can be under-detected; distances over many one-hot columns are hard to interpret.
- *Support vector machine (RBF)* (classification): Flexible non-linear boundary with margin-based regularisation. Outputs decision scores, not probabilities (ROC-AUC uses the scores); least interpretable model here.
- *Random forest* (classification): Robust to noise and interactions; usually strong ranking (ROC-AUC). Less transparent; probabilities from vote shares are not calibrated.

## Limitations

- One held-out test set per cohort (79 and 130 students). Test scores are single estimates with wide uncertainty; the nested-CV means and SDs are the more stable summary.
- G1 and G2 are interim grades of the same school year. The full-history and mid-year scenarios reflect how much the final grade is already known from them, not a better understanding of students.
- Early-scenario results describe what background, behavioural and school attributes are associated with; they are not causal and not a prediction of dropout.
- Both cohorts come from two schools in one region and one year. Performance on other schools or years is unknown.
- Students with G3 = 0 are kept in every result. Every one of them has 0 recorded absences (Phase 4), which suggests a G3 of 0 can mean the final evaluation was not taken; the sensitivity table shows how much they weigh on regression error.
- Classification uses each estimator's default decision rule. A different threshold trades recall against precision; the threshold was not tuned.
